# Example: a board deck condensed into an email

Source: the Acme 2026 growth strategy deck (the worked example of the `mbb-deck` skill, `examples/sample-storyline.json`). The strategy team needs the email that sends the deck to the board ahead of the April meeting.

## Step 1 - the titles, in order

1. Acme should move upmarket to enterprise HR in 2026, funded by sunsetting SMB, adding an estimated $40M ARR over 24 months *(executive summary)*
2. Growth has stalled at 3% for two years while three competitors have grown 25%+
3. The enterprise segment is $4.2B and growing 18%, three times our current addressable market
4. Three of our five product lines fall in the low-growth, low-margin quadrant
5. Of four growth paths, only enterprise expansion meets all three criteria
6. Enterprise expansion is the only option that scores high on market size, fit, and feasibility
7. The path to enterprise-ready spans four sequential capabilities, deliverable in three quarters
8. The SMB tier costs $14M annually to serve and is contribution-margin negative in 60% of accounts
9. Combined: enterprise expansion adds $40M ARR by end-2027; sunsetting SMB frees $14M to fund it
10. Implementation in three phases over 18 months, with the SMB sunset starting in Q2 2026
11. The board is asked for three decisions in April 2026 so that the build starts in Q2 2026

## Step 2 - titles-only read

The titles tell the story: growth has stalled, enterprise is the larger market and the only path that meets the criteria, the product can get there in three quarters, the SMB tier pays for it, and three decisions are needed. Titles 5 and 6 make the same point twice; the summary uses it once.

## Step 3 - governing thought

Taken from the deck unchanged: *Acme should move upmarket to enterprise HR in 2026, funded by sunsetting the SMB tier, adding an estimated $40M ARR over 24 months.*

## Step 4 - the email

> **Subject:** Decision needed at the April board meeting: move upmarket to enterprise HR, funded by the SMB sunset
>
> Acme has been a top-3 player in US mid-market HR software for five years, but growth has stalled at 3% for two years while three competitors have grown 25%+ by moving upmarket.
>
> **Recommendation: move upmarket to enterprise HR in 2026, funded by sunsetting the SMB tier, adding an estimated $40M ARR over 24 months.**
>
> - **The enterprise segment is the larger market** - $4.2B and growing 18%, three times our current addressable market
> - **Enterprise expansion is the only path that meets all three criteria** - of four growth paths compared on market size, fit and feasibility
> - **The product gap closes in three quarters** - four sequential capabilities with current engineering capacity
> - **The SMB tier funds the move** - it costs $14M annually to serve and is contribution-margin negative in 60% of accounts
>
> Decisions needed at the April 2026 board meeting:
> 1. Approve the move upmarket to enterprise HR as the 2026 growth priority
> 2. Approve the SMB tier sunset over 18 months, starting in Q2 2026
> 3. Release the budget for 3 enterprise sales hires in Q2 2026
>
> Why now: the 9-month feature lead over competitors holds only if the build starts in Q2 2026.
>
> The deck is attached. The decisions page pairs each decision with its safeguard; for the SMB sunset that is the 18-month transition period and the partner referral program.

## Step 5 - number check

Every figure in the email, traced to the deck:

| Figure | In the email | In the deck |
|---|---|---|
| 3% growth, two years | context | executive summary, growth page |
| 25%+ competitor growth | context | executive summary, growth page |
| $40M ARR over 24 months | recommendation | executive summary title, governing thought |
| $4.2B, 18% | bullet 1 | market page |
| four paths, three criteria | bullet 2 | options page |
| three quarters, four capabilities | bullet 3 | capabilities page |
| $14M, 60% | bullet 4 | SMB page |
| 18 months, Q2 2026, 3 hires | decisions | decisions page |
| 9-month feature lead | why now | decisions page, risk page |

One point raised with the author instead of being smoothed over: the synthesis page's title says the move "adds $40M ARR", while its caption describes $26M of net new ARR plus $14M of cost savings as "a total $40M EBITDA improvement". ARR and EBITDA are different measures. The email uses the governing thought's wording and the author is asked which is meant.

## Why this works

- **Four bullets from ten pages.** The summary groups the pages into the deck's four arguments; it does not list one bullet per slide.
- **Nothing is added.** Every bullet has a page behind it and every decision appears on the decisions page with the same wording.
- **The subject line carries the ask and the date.**
- **The main concern is answered in the last line,** with a pointer to the page by name.
