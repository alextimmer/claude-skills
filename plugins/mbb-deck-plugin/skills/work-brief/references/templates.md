# Brief templates

Fill in what applies and delete the rest. Square brackets are for the sender to replace; a bracket left in the final brief is a gap the recipient will have to guess.

## For an AI tool

```
You are [role], writing for [audience: who they are, what they already know, their main concern].

Objective: after reading, the audience should [action], [by when].

Key message: [the governing thought in one sentence].

Structure:
- [Opening: situation and complication in two sentences]
- [One bullet per argument, each with its key number; at most N]
- [Close: the decisions needed, with deadline and why now]
Length: [words or pages]. Format: [email / one page / slides outline]. Language: [..].

Facts and limits:
- Use only [the attached files / the text below]. Do not use other knowledge for facts or figures.
- Figures are stated against [reference: budget, prior year, benchmark], for [period].
- Do not invent numbers. List every figure you needed and could not find.
- Fixed, do not change: [key message, named decisions, named figures].
- Open, your judgment is wanted: [wording, order of arguments, which evidence to show].

First return [the storyline as bullets with one sub-level]. Wait for confirmation before writing the full text.
```

## For a colleague or analyst

```
What I need: [deliverable, format, length], by [date]. First checkpoint: [storyline / outline] by [date].

Why: [the decision or meeting this serves, and what depends on it].

Who it is for: [audience, what they know, their main concern].
What they should do afterwards: [objective].
What we want to tell them: [key message, or for an analysis brief: the question to answer].

How it should be built: [structure; the template to use].
What to work from: [sources]. Figures against [reference], for [period].

Already decided, please do not reopen: [..]
Open, your call: [..]
What good looks like: [two or three properties].

Come back to me if: [the conditions under which they should stop and ask].
```

## For an agency or contractor

```
Project: [name]. Contact: [one person]. Approval: [who signs off, in how many rounds].

Deliverable: [what, format, file type, length, language, template or brand rules].
Deadline: [final]. Milestones: [storyline / first draft / final], each with a date.

Audience: [..]. Objective: [..]. Key message: [..].
Structure: [..].

In scope: [..]
Out of scope: [..]

Material provided: [list]. Use only this material for facts and figures; figures are stated against [reference].
Confidentiality and reuse: [what may be kept, shared or shown].

What good looks like: [..]
```

## Analysis brief (the answer is not known yet)

Use this when the key message cannot be stated. The question takes its place.

```
Question to answer: [one sentence].
Why it matters now: [the complication].
Who will act on the answer: [audience], to decide [decision], by [date].

Current hypothesis, if any: [..]. It is a hypothesis: say so if the evidence points elsewhere.

What to examine: [the breakdown: by which one dimension].
What to work from: [sources]. Figures against [reference], for [period].

Return: [the answer in one sentence, three supporting findings, what remains uncertain, and the next step].
Do not return a report of what was done.
```

## Before sending

- Can the recipient start without calling you?
- Is every bracket replaced, or deliberately marked as open?
- Are the audience, the objective and the key message yours, in your words?
- Is it clear what must not be changed?
- Is there a checkpoint before the full result?
