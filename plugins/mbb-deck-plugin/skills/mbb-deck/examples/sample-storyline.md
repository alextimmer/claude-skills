# Example: A complete storyline before slide-building

This is what the output of Step 2 (SCQA) and Step 3 (action-title outline) should look like before any slide content is built.

## Hypothetical brief

Acme Corp, a US mid-market SaaS company, has flat revenue for two consecutive years. The CEO has asked the strategy team for a recommendation on where to focus growth investment in 2026. The board meeting is in three weeks.

## SCQA

- **Situation:** Acme has been a top-3 player in US mid-market HR software for the past five years, with $180M ARR and ~25% gross margin.
- **Complication:** Revenue growth has stalled at 3% for two years, while three competitors have grown 25%+ by entering enterprise and adjacent product categories.
- **Question:** Where should Acme invest its 2026 growth budget to restart double-digit growth?
- **Answer (governing thought):** Acme should invest its 2026 growth budget in moving upmarket to enterprise HR, funded by sunsetting the underperforming SMB tier — a path that adds an estimated $40M ARR over 24 months.

## Action-title outline (slide-by-slide)

1. *Title slide* — "Acme 2026 Growth Strategy: Recommendation to the Board"
2. **Acme should move upmarket to enterprise HR in 2026, funded by sunsetting SMB, adding an estimated $40M ARR over 24 months** *(executive summary)*
3. **Growth has stalled at 3% for two years while three competitors have grown 25%+ by moving upmarket** *(situation + complication)*
4. **The growth gap is driven by share loss in mid-market accounts above 500 employees, not by pricing** *(diagnosis)*
5. **We evaluated four growth paths against three criteria; only enterprise expansion meets all three** *(approach — comparison table)*
6. **The enterprise segment is $4.2B and growing 18% — three times our current addressable market** *(finding 1: market size)*
7. **Two of our top-30 customers already use Acme above the 500-employee threshold and would expand within 12 months** *(finding 2: existing demand signal)*
8. **The product gap to enterprise-ready is six features, deliverable in three quarters with current engineering capacity** *(finding 3: feasibility)*
9. **The SMB tier costs $14M annually to serve and contributes negative contribution margin in 60% of accounts** *(finding 4: source of funding)*
10. **Combined: enterprise expansion adds $40M ARR by end-2027; sunsetting SMB frees $14M to fund it** *(synthesis — restates the recommendation with full backing)*
11. **Implementation: three phases over 18 months, with the SMB sunset starting in Q2 2026** *(roadmap)*
12. **Key risks and mitigations: enterprise sales cycle, SMB customer churn, competitive response** *(risks)*
13. *Appendix divider*
14. *(Appendix: detailed financials, competitor benchmark, customer interview summary)*

## Why this outline works

- **Read top-to-bottom, the titles tell the whole story.** Slides 2–10 alone are sufficient for a board member to grasp the recommendation and its rationale without seeing any slide bodies.
- **Slide 2 (executive summary) restates the governing thought verbatim.** Slide 10 restates it again with full backing. The recommendation appears three times: once up front, once at the synthesis, once at the title slide framing.
- **Findings are MECE.** Slides 6–9 cover (a) market size, (b) demand signal, (c) feasibility, (d) source of funding. Together they answer "why this, why now, and how do we pay for it" without overlap.
- **No topic titles.** Every title is a complete sentence with a verb and a specific claim.
- **Implementation comes after the recommendation is fully argued, not before.**

## What to do with this outline

Show it to the user. Do not start building slide bodies until they confirm the storyline. This is the most valuable review point in the entire deck-building process.
